### The neural era, and the map of the field

<small>(~40 min) · reading: <a href="../../textbook/history-of-graphics.html#era-neural">History, Sections 8 to 10</a></small>


---

## What if the scene is learned?

Every era so far stored a scene **explicitly**, and each changed what the stored thing is:

- lines (era 1) · triangles (era 2) · patches and micropolygons (era 3) · scanned points and voxels (era 6)
- the neural era stores a scene as **what explains its photographs**
- the enabler: **differentiable rendering**, a renderer whose output image has a gradient with respect to the scene

"Which scene explains these photographs?" becomes an **optimization**: render, compare to the photos, nudge the scene, repeat.


---

## NeRF: a scene as a function (2020)

**Neural radiance fields** (Mildenhall et al., Berkeley and Google): a small network answers one question.

```text
   query:   position (x, y, z)  +  viewing direction
   answer:  color (r, g, b)     +  density (how much stuff is here)
```

- render by marching each pixel's ray through the field and accumulating color: the **volume rendering integral**, the one medical imaging used for CT scans in the 1980s
- trained from a few dozen ordinary photographs; renders viewpoints **nobody photographed**
- the catch: seconds to minutes per frame, days to train; **Instant-NGP** (NVIDIA, 2022) cut training to seconds with a hash table of learned features


---

## Gaussian splatting (2023)

**3D Gaussian splatting** (Kerbl, Kopanas, Leimkühler, Drettakis, Inria): not a network, **millions of translucent blobs**.

- each Gaussian: a position, a shape (an ellipsoid), a color, an opacity
- optimized until the blobs, drawn back to front, reproduce the photographs
- drawing them is **rasterization**: project each blob to the screen and blend its footprint
- result: learned scenes at **real-time** frame rates, in a browser

## Learned images: three older ideas underneath

- a **neural network** is a function with adjustable weights, trained by nudging every weight to reduce the error on examples (backpropagation, 1986); with enough units it approximates essentially any function (1989, 1991), and it is **smooth between its examples**
- an **embedding** is a learned vector that stands for a thing, with similar things placed near each other (word2vec, 2013); an **encoder** maps to it, a **decoder** maps back (the autoencoder, 2006)
- **CLIP** (2021) trains an image encoder and a text encoder together, so a photo and its caption land on the **same point**: words and pictures share one coordinate system

## Diffusion: destroy, then learn to undo

```text
   forward (fixed, known):   image ──add a little noise──▶ ... ──▶ pure noise
   reverse (learned):        pure noise ──denoise one step──▶ ... ──▶ a new image
```

- train a network to undo **one small step** of a noise process you control
- training is a **regression** against noise the trainer drew itself, which is why it is stable
- sampling is a walk: ~1,000 steps originally (**DDPM**, 2020), a few dozen (**DDIM**), a handful after distillation
- found three times: thermodynamics (2015), score matching (2019), variational inference (2020); beat the adversarial models by 2021

## Three steps to the tools everyone uses

- **latent diffusion** (Stable Diffusion, 2022): diffuse a compressed code, decode once at the end; a 512 × 512 × 3 image is 786,432 numbers, its 64 × 64 × 4 latent is 16,384, a factor of **48**
- **conditioning**: a text embedding (CLIP, or a language model's) steers every step; **classifier-free guidance** exaggerates the prompt's pull by a scale the user sets
- the denoiser moves from a U-Net to a **transformer** on image patches (2023): the recipe unchanged, quality following scale


---

## Where the generator meets the pipeline

- **ControlNet** (2023): condition the image model on the pipeline's own by-products (a depth buffer, a normal map, an edge image); geometry from the renderer, appearance from the model
- **score distillation** (DreamFusion, 2022): an image model **trains a 3D scene**, judging renders from random viewpoints through a differentiable renderer
- **video** (2022, minute-long clips by 2024): the same recipe with a time axis
- what still breaks is what a scene would enforce: object permanence, physics, counting, legible text, a camera you can move

## The representation changes; the craft does not

- lines, triangles, patches, points and voxels, Gaussians and network weights: each announced as the end of the last, **each still in use**
- the camera matrix projects a Gaussian the way it projects a vertex
- the volume integral that renders a NeRF is the one that rendered a CT scan in 1988
- the rasterizer that draws a splat is the raster machines' rasterizer
- the sampling theory behind a jagged 1977 edge governs a 2023 path tracer's noise

## Reading a graphics paper

1. abstract, then **every figure and caption** in order: graphics papers carry the argument in their figures
2. the results: what exactly is compared, and is the baseline fair?
3. only if it still matters: the method, with a pencil, re-deriving what you can
4. follow the citations **backward**; check for a **project page** (video, code, data)

Start with Sketchpad (1963) or "The Ultimate Display" (1965); the history chapter links every paper it cites.
