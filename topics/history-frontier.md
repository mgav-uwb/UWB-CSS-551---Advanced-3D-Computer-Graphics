## A scene learned from photographs

<div class="cols stack"><div class="txt">

- **NeRF**, neural radiance fields (2020, Ben Mildenhall, Pratul Srinivasan, Matthew Tancik, Jonathan Barron, Ravi Ramamoorthi, Ren Ng; UC Berkeley, Google, UC San Diego): a network trained on photographs gives color and density at any point and direction, rendered with the volume integral of 1988
- **Instant neural graphics primitives** (2022, Thomas Müller, Alex Evans, Christoph Schied, Alexander Keller, NVIDIA): training from hours to seconds
- **3D Gaussian splatting** (2023, Bernhard Kerbl, Georgios Kopanas, Thomas Leimkühler, George Drettakis; Inria, Université Côte d'Azur, Max Planck Institute for Informatics): millions of translucent Gaussians, rasterized at 30 frames a second or more at 1080p

<span class="cost">**Lag:** radiance fields, 2020 paper to real-time splats (2023), 3 years.</span>

## Pixels the renderer never computed

<div class="cols"><div class="txt">

- NVIDIA's **Deep Learning Super Sampling (DLSS)**, introduced with Turing in 2018: fewer pixels rendered, a network on the tensor cores fills them in
- **DLSS 2.0** (23 March 2020): renders "one quarter to one half of the pixels"
- **DLSS 3** (20 September 2022): generates whole frames
- **DLSS 4** (6 January 2025): transformer networks

## What learned graphics may replace

- a learned scene needs **no modeled geometry**, **no hand-set materials** and **no light-transport solver**
- an upscaler or a frame generator supplies pixels the renderer never computed
- a diffusion model draws an image from text
- so much of what the problems decks named **may become obsolete**
- what stays: a camera matrix projects a Gaussian as it projects a vertex; **splatting is rasterization**; NeRF evaluates the volume integral of 1988
