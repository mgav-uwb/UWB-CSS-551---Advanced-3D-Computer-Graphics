  2. Blender (blender.org): render one scene in EEVEE and in Cycles and compare.
  3. The Book of Shaders, then Shadertoy: write a fragment shader in the browser.
  4. three.js examples (threejs.org): scene graphs and viewing in the browser, the stack the course demos use.
  5. pbr-book.org: read chapter 1 of the free 4th edition.
  6. Poly Haven and the Stanford 3D Scanning Repository: free HDR environments, textures and the bunny.
  7. Ke-Sen Huang’s lists: find this year’s papers from SIGGRAPH (the ACM Special Interest Group on Computer Graphics and Interactive Techniques) with free PDFs.
How to read a SIGGRAPH paper First pass: the abstract, then every figure and caption in order (graphics papers carry most of their argument in figures), then the results: what exactly is compared, and is the baseline fair? Second pass, only if the paper still matters: the method, with a pencil. Follow citations backward from a modern paper’s related work to the papers in the history chapter. Look for the project page, which usually has video, code and data.
